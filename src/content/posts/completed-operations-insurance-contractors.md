General liability insurance is the first policy every contractor is told to buy. But there's a critical piece of that GL that most agents shortchange: completed-operations coverage. It's the part of your GL that protects you *after* the project is finished — and it's where the most expensive contractor claims actually happen.

## What Is Completed-Operations Coverage?

Your GL policy has two distinct liability sections:

**Premises and operations:** Covers bodily injury and property damage that happens *while you're working* on a project — a visitor trips over your equipment, you back your truck into a client's fence, a worker knocks over a structural element.

**Completed operations:** Covers bodily injury and property damage that happens *after you're done* — claims arising from work you completed. A roof leaks six months after punch-out. A deck collapses eighteen months after installation. Wiring you ran causes a fire two years later.

Completed operations is a separate coverage section with its own aggregate limit. Many contractors — and many agents — underwrite it wrong or don't prioritize it. That's a serious problem because the completed-operations section is where the big claims live.

## Why Completed-Operations Claims Are So Common

When you're physically on the job, you can see issues and address them. The risk of an immediate premises-and-operations claim is manageable.

When the job is done, you have no visibility. A subtle waterproofing failure. An improper structural connection. A HVAC installation that didn't quite follow spec. These issues take time to manifest — sometimes months, sometimes years. By the time the claim arrives, you may have changed carriers, lapsed coverage, or changed business entities.

This is why completed-operations coverage matters so much:

1. **The claim arrives long after the job closes.** Property damage from faulty work often isn't discovered until the next rain, the next freeze cycle, or the next structural load test. Claims filed two or three years after the work was done are routine in construction.

2. **Defense costs are enormous even when you win.** A contractor named in a construction defect suit pays legal costs whether or not they're ultimately found liable. Defense can run $50,000–$200,000 before a judgment is reached. Without GL, those costs come out of the business.

3. **The statute of limitations is long.** In most states, construction defect claims can be filed years after work is completed. In some states, the clock doesn't even start until the defect is discovered. Your completed-operations coverage needs to be structured for a long tail.

## Occurrence vs. Claims-Made: The Most Important GL Decision

This is where most contractors get hurt. There are two GL policy forms:

**Occurrence:** Covers claims arising from work done during the policy period, regardless of when the claim is filed. If you did the work in 2022 and the claim is filed in 2025, your 2022 policy responds.

**Claims-made:** Covers claims filed while the policy is active. If you did the work in 2022 and you've switched carriers or let the policy lapse by the time the 2025 claim is filed, your current policy may not cover it — and your old policy only covers claims filed while it was active.

For contractors with completed-operations exposure — which is essentially every contractor — **occurrence-based GL is strongly preferred.** A claims-made GL creates a tail risk that is difficult to manage and dangerous to ignore.

When you switch carriers with a claims-made policy, you need to purchase an extended reporting period (tail coverage) to maintain protection for work already completed. This is an added cost that occurrence policies simply don't require.

## The Completed-Operations Aggregate Limit

Your GL policy has two separate aggregate limits:

- **General aggregate:** Covers premises/operations claims, advertising injury, personal injury
- **Products-completed operations aggregate:** Covers your completed-operations claims

These are separate pools. Exhaust one and the other is still available. But if your completed-operations aggregate is set too low — or if your agent placed a policy with a low or non-existent completed-operations limit — you can run out of coverage in the middle of a construction defect claim.

Standard limits for residential contractors are often $1M per occurrence / $2M aggregate with a matching $2M completed-operations aggregate. Larger commercial contractors with higher project values often carry $2M/$4M with umbrella above. Review your completed-operations aggregate against the size and scope of your typical projects.

## Subcontractor Work and Completed Operations

If you use subcontractors, there's another layer of risk: your GL policy may contain a **subcontractor exclusion** that removes coverage for work performed by subs.

This matters enormously for completed-operations claims. A defect in a sub's work that damages a completed project can generate a claim against you — the general contractor — even when the fault lies with the sub. If your GL excludes subcontractor work, you're uninsured for that claim.

Before binding a GL policy, confirm:
- Does the policy contain a subcontractor exclusion?
- If so, does completing certificates from your subs remove the exclusion?
- Is completed-operations coverage maintained for work done by subs under your supervision?

## What Proper Completed-Operations Coverage Looks Like

A well-structured GL for a contractor with completed-operations exposure includes:

- **Occurrence form** — not claims-made
- **Meaningful completed-operations aggregate** — matched to your project values and exposure
- **No subcontractor exclusion** — or a workaround that doesn't leave you exposed for sub work
- **Defense outside limits** — some policies erode the aggregate with defense costs; others (often more expensive) pay defense outside limits, preserving the full aggregate for judgments
- **Coverage for damage to completed work** — the GL policy's property damage coverage for work you've completed (not to be confused with a builder's risk policy active during construction)

## The Bottom Line for Contractors

Your on-site work is visible. Your completed work is not. The biggest liability exposure in contracting — by dollar value and frequency — comes from work that's already done and problems that won't surface for months or years.

Occurrence-based GL with a properly funded completed-operations aggregate isn't optional. It's the core protection for the way construction actually works.
