A contractor's tools are their livelihood. A full day of missing tools means a full day of stopped work. A tools theft on a jobsite — increasingly common in active construction markets — can mean $10,000, $30,000, or more in losses overnight.

Most contractors assume their tools are covered under their commercial property or business owner's policy. Usually, they're not. Here's why, and what to do about it.

## Why Commercial Property Doesn't Cover Your Tools Properly

Standard commercial property insurance covers business property at a **fixed, scheduled location** — your shop, your warehouse, your office. The coverage is location-based.

Your tools, by definition, travel. They're in your trucks, in your employees' vehicles, at multiple jobsites simultaneously. Many commercial property policies include an off-premises coverage extension — but it's typically limited to 10% of the building limit, and it may only apply to temporary storage, not active use at jobsites.

If you have a $300,000 commercial property policy and your tools are worth $80,000, your off-premises coverage is $30,000 — and only if the policy doesn't have specific exclusions for property at jobsites or in transit.

A claim for tools stolen from a locked truck overnight at a jobsite? Often denied under commercial property.

## What Inland Marine Tools and Equipment Coverage Does

The coverage designed for contractor tools is called **inland marine insurance** — specifically, a tools and equipment (contractor's equipment) inland marine policy. Despite the name, it has nothing to do with boats. Inland marine insurance was developed in the early 20th century to cover property in transit across land — and tools that travel to jobsites are exactly what it was designed for.

An inland marine tools policy covers your tools and equipment:

- At any jobsite
- In locked vehicles (trucks, vans, trailers)
- In transit between locations
- At your shop or storage facility

It follows the tools wherever they go. There's no fixed-location limitation.

## What's Covered Under a Tools Policy

Coverage varies by form, but standard inland marine tools policies cover:

**Theft** — including theft from locked vehicles and locked jobsite storage boxes. This is typically the highest-frequency claim type.

**Accidental damage and breakage** — tools dropped, run over, or damaged on the job.

**Fire** — tools destroyed in a vehicle or trailer fire.

**Vandalism** — tools damaged intentionally.

**Mysterious disappearance** — some forms cover loss when a tool simply can't be accounted for without evidence of a specific theft or damage event.

**Transit losses** — tools damaged or lost while being transported between sites.

## Blanket vs. Scheduled Coverage

Tools policies are typically written as one of two structures:

**Blanket coverage** sets a total dollar value for your entire tool and equipment inventory. If you have $50,000 worth of tools, you carry a $50,000 blanket limit. Individual items below a threshold (often $1,000–$2,500) don't need to be individually listed — the blanket applies.

**Scheduled coverage** lists individual items — typically tools worth more than the threshold — with their own stated values. High-value items like excavators, large generators, laser levels, or specialized trade equipment are often scheduled separately.

Most contractor tools programs use a **combination approach**: a blanket limit for the general tool inventory, with high-value items (above $1,500–$5,000) scheduled individually. This prevents a single expensive-item loss from consuming the entire blanket limit.

## What Tools Are Worth Covering

The threshold question for tool coverage is: what's the replacement cost of what you'd lose if a vehicle was broken into or a jobsite storage box was taken tonight?

For most active contractors, the answer is higher than expected:

- Power tools — circular saws, drills, impact drivers — $500–$2,000+ each
- Pneumatic tools and compressors — $1,000–$5,000
- Laser levels and measuring equipment — $1,000–$5,000
- Specialty trade tools — significantly more for the right trades
- Generators — $2,000–$15,000
- Tool trailers and their contents — easily $20,000–$50,000

An electrical contractor with two vans fully stocked for residential service work could easily have $60,000–$100,000 in tools and equipment at risk. A roofing crew's trailer inventory can run $40,000+. A plumbing supply van can easily exceed $30,000 in tools and fittings.

## The Deductible Question

Tools policies typically carry a per-occurrence deductible of $250–$2,500. Higher deductibles reduce premium; lower deductibles reduce out-of-pocket cost per claim.

Unlike commercial property with its building deductibles, tools claim deductibles are manageable. A $500 deductible on a $15,000 theft claim is well worth the trade-off vs. self-insuring the entire loss.

## Documentation: The Key to Fast Claims

Tools claims are paid based on documentation. A carrier asking you to substantiate a $25,000 tools theft claim will want:

- A list of stolen items with descriptions and approximate values
- Serial numbers where available
- Purchase records or receipts
- Police report (usually required for theft claims)
- Photos if available

The contractors who get paid quickly and fully are the ones who've kept a current tools inventory with serial numbers and approximate replacement costs. The contractors who struggle at claim time are the ones who can't tell the adjuster what they had.

Creating and maintaining a basic tools inventory — even a spreadsheet — is one of the highest-value pre-loss activities a contractor can do.

## How Tools Coverage Coordinates With Commercial Auto

One question that comes up frequently: if tools are stolen from my truck, does commercial auto cover it?

**No.** Commercial auto covers the vehicle and third-party liability from accidents. It does not cover the contents of the vehicle — tools, equipment, materials, supplies. Tools in your truck need tools coverage; the truck itself is covered by commercial auto.
